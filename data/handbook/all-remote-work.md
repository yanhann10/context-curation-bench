# All-Remote Work: Async, Handbook-First, Non-Linear Workday

**Source:** https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/company/culture/all-remote/ (and /handbook-first/, /asynchronous/, /non-linear-workday/)
**Fetched:** 2026-04-18

## What "all-remote" means at GitLab

GitLab is one of the world's largest **all-remote** companies. GitLab uses the term "all-remote" (not hybrid or distributed) to describe its 100% remote workforce across 65+ countries. There is no GitLab office — all team members work from wherever they are.

## Handbook-First Communication

GitLab is intentional about documenting in a manner that creates a **single source of truth**. The company operates **handbook-first**, and in valuing transparency, makes its handbook publicly accessible.

### Terminology

At GitLab, documentation is written **before** the change is communicated. You document the solution first, then announce via Slack or email. This way there is no post-change documentation step that gets skipped.

The goal is not "documentation for its own sake" — it's that **net new solutions are documented in a universally accessible handbook before dissemination**, and iterations are made to the single source of truth.

### Why handbook-first matters

- As a team scales, the need for documentation increases in parallel with the cost of not doing it.
- A handbook-first organization benefits from a single source of truth.
- Without structured documentation, teams re-ask the same questions and lose to interruptions and suboptimal knowledge transfers.

### Slack retention policy — a forcing function

**Only 90 days of Slack activity is retained.** After 90 days, it's gone. This is intentional — it prevents Slack from becoming a project-management tool and forces team members to document in the handbook.

Discussions begin in **GitLab Issues or Merge Requests**, not in Slack. This ensures that whatever is merged into the handbook has a proper trail.

Slack is for **informal communication** (chat, quick questions) only.

## Asynchronous Work

GitLab provides extensive guidance on embracing asynchronous communication. Async work allows team members across time zones to contribute effectively without requiring real-time presence.

### Core async practices

- Use GitLab Issues and Merge Requests for discussions, not meetings.
- Make meeting agendas and notes available async — ideally in a shared Google Doc linked from the calendar invite.
- Record synchronous meetings and share with the broader team.
- Live-document meetings: participants type into the shared doc during the meeting, contributing asynchronously and synchronously.
- Avoid calendar tetris. Favor writing over real-time meetings.

### Async benefits

- Enables a global workforce across time zones.
- Reduces meeting burden.
- Creates a written trail — perfect for compliance and onboarding.
- Supports deep work and mental health (less interruption).
- Bridges the knowledge gap — written history is searchable and distributable.

## Non-Linear Workday

GitLab supports a **non-linear workday** — the idea that remote workers may not work 9-to-5 in a single block, and that's fine as long as results are delivered.

- **Flexible working hours** mean you can step away when needed without guilt.
- Regular life activities — going to the gym, taking a nap, grocery shopping, helping loved ones — are part of normal work-life integration.
- No glorification of overwork. Not taking time off is viewed as a weakness, not a strength.
- GitLab measures **impact, not activity** (see the TeamOps framework at `/teamops/measurement-clarity/#measure-impact-not-activity`).

## Expectations for All-Remote Team Members

### Communication expectations

- **Write things down first.** Don't bury knowledge in 1:1 conversations or Slack DMs.
- **Handbook-first.** If a decision is made, update the handbook. If the handbook is wrong, fix it via MR.
- **Transparency by default.** Default to public channels (both within GitLab Slack and within the public handbook).
- **Notify early for PTO.** Give advance notice at least **2× as many days as the planned time off** (e.g., 6 business days for 3 days off).

### Time zone expectations

- Respect time zones in meeting scheduling — rotate meeting times so the pain is shared.
- Async-first: don't require synchronous attendance unless critical.
- Use tools like `worldtimebuddy.com` for scheduling across regions.

### On-call expectations

- On-call is coordinated and documented for the engineering on-call rotation (see `/handbook/engineering/on-call/`).
- Scheduled leave for on-call team members follows the coverage-handoff process documented in each oncall page.

## Remote Onboarding Expectations

- **Take at least 2 full weeks** for onboarding before starting team-specific work in week 3.
- Use the onboarding issue template as a self-service, self-driven learning flow.
- Dogfood GitLab — all onboarding tasks are completed in GitLab Issues.
- Early social connection is critical — join `#new_team_members` and introduce yourself on day 1.

## Key Principles

1. **Handbook-first** — document before announcing.
2. **Asynchronous by default** — meetings are the exception, not the rule.
3. **Single source of truth** — the handbook. If it's not in the handbook, it's not policy.
4. **Iteration** — handbooks are never finished.
5. **Transparency** — public by default.
6. **Results, not activity** — measure impact, not hours.
7. **Bias toward asynchronous communication** — give others time to process before expecting a response.
